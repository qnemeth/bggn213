Background

In this lab we will use AlphaFold to predict protein structure from sequence.

Multiple sequence alignments (MSAs) are the key inputs to AlphaFold. The neural network uses co-evolution as restraints in folding simulations. Co-evolution is predictive of contacts (i.e. distance restraints). Contacts in proteins are evolutionarily conserved and encoded in an MSA due to co-evolution.

Physics-Based Approach

The core idea is that you can describe the energy of something based on its shape. Potential functions describe a system’s energy as a function of its structure. This approach calculates the potential energy surface that fully describes the energy of a molecular system as a function of its geometry.

Knowledge-Based Approach

The basic idea is that we can use probability as a proxy for energy. It uses things we have seen a lot to determine what we might see in similar things. It is limited by what you’ve seen before.

AlphaFold built on existing knowledge-based approaches and combined them with physics. For a give sequence, it references input MSA/templates, and then finds the structure with the lowest free energy.
